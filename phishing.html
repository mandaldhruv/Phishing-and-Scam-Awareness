Phishing Awareness and Detection

Learn how spoofed emails, counterfeit websites, and SMS lures trick users, and understand the anatomical red flags hidden inside deceptive messages.

Delivery Channels

The Four Primary Forms of Phishing

Attackers adapt their delivery method depending on user devices and the information they intend to harvest.

Email Phishing

Mass emails spoofing recognized companies (banks, cloud services, streaming portals) designed to direct recipients to fake login pages.

Smishing (SMS)

Short text messages warning of courier issues, traffic fines, or electricity disconnection with shortened links (bit.ly, tinyurl).

Vishing (Voice)

Live phone calls where the fraudster impersonates law enforcement, telecom operators, or bank security teams to extract OTPs.

Clone Phishing

Legitimate emails previously sent are copied by attackers, with authentic attachments or links swapped for malicious lookalikes.

Educational Demonstration

Anatomy of a Phishing Message

Examine this simulated fraudulent communication. Notice how multiple subtle indicators reveal the deception.

Educational Simulation From: State Bank Security Alert <security-alert@sbi-online-service29.tk>
Date: Today, 08:42 AM
Subject: [CRITICAL] Your Internet Banking Access Will Be Terminated in 12 Hours
Dear Valued Customer,

Our automated monitoring system detected suspicious login attempts on your net banking profile from an unauthorized IP address in Singapore. As per Reserve Bank regulations, your access has been temporarily restricted.

To restore full account functionality and prevent permanent cancellation, you must confirm your KYC identification details immediately by clicking the official security link below:

http://sbi-online-service29.tk/login/verify-credentials.php

Failure to complete this verification within 12 hours will result in permanent debit card suspension and a penalty fee of Rs. 1,200.

Sincerely,
Central Security & Compliance Team
Red Flag 01

Deceptive Sender Address

While the display name says "State Bank", the actual domain is sbi-online-service29.tk. Official banks operate on verified registered domains (e.g. sbi.co.in or onlinesbi.sbi), never on free .tk extensions.

Red Flag 02

Manufactured Panic

Phrases like "Will Be Terminated in 12 Hours" and threats of penalty fees bypass rational thought. Legitimate financial institutions never use countdown threats to force login entries.

Red Flag 03

Insecure & Spoofed Link

The hyperlink uses unencrypted http:// rather than https, and leads to an arbitrary server path rather than the genuine bank portal.

Detection Checklist

Six Red Flags to Look For

01

Mismatched or Lookalike Domains

Carefully inspect the letters in the domain name. Scammers use typosquatting such as replacing "o" with "0", "l" with "1", or adding words like "support-bank.com" instead of "bank.com".

02

Generic or Missing Greetings

Legitimate organizations where you hold an account usually address you by your registered name or account number prefix. Be cautious of emails starting with "Dear Valued Customer" or "Dear User".

03

Demands for Passwords or PINs

No genuine customer support agent or security alert requires you to send your password, CVV, or one-time password back in an email, form, or chat window.

04

Unexpected Attachments

Be extremely cautious of unexpected files ending in .exe, .scr, .zip, or .iso. Even invoices sent as Word or Excel files (.docm, .xlsm) can contain malicious automated macro scripts.

05

Spelling and Grammatical Errors

Poor phrasing, irregular capitalization, or awkward translations frequently occur in mass fraudulent campaigns launched from automated templates.

06

Sense of Uncompromising Urgency

Whenever a message warns that your funds will disappear, legal action will begin, or your account will close in minutes, pause immediately. Urgency is the fraudster's strongest weapon.

Practical Rule

How to Safely Handle Suspicious Messages

If an email or SMS raises even the slightest doubt:

  1. Do not click any embedded link or open attachments.
  2. Do not reply to the message or call phone numbers mentioned in the message body.
  3. Open your web browser manually, navigate to the official website from a trusted bookmark, and log in to inspect your account notifications.
  4. Contact the organization via the official customer support telephone number printed on your physical card or monthly bank statement.
