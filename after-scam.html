Post-Incident Emergency Action Guide

If you or someone you know has transferred funds or revealed credentials to a fraudster, immediate calm action during the "Golden Hour" can prevent further unauthorized debits and assist fund recovery.

Urgent Priority (First 1 to 2 Hours)

Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline: 1930

In India, dialing 1930 immediately connects you with the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting System. Reporting promptly allows law enforcement to place a temporary lien or freeze on the beneficiary bank account or digital wallet before the fraudsters withdraw the cash.

Incident Protocol

The 7-Step Post-Incident Response

01

Stop All Further Communication

Immediately disconnect the phone call or chat session. Scammers often keep victims on the phone to create artificial panic, prevent them from contacting their bank, or attempt secondary debits under the guise of "reversing the initial error".

02

Freeze Cards and Payment Access

Open your official mobile banking application and toggle off your debit card, credit card, and international transaction permissions. Use the instant "Hotlist Card" or "Lock Net Banking" feature if available.

03

Notify Your Bank Fraud Department

Call the verified 24x7 customer support number printed on the back of your bank card. Inform them that an unauthorized transaction has occurred. Request a written complaint number (acknowledgement reference) for your records.

04

Preserve All Digital Evidence

Do not delete chats, emails, or call logs. Take clear screenshots showing:

  • Transaction reference IDs (UTR numbers)
  • Sender phone numbers and profile handles
  • Exact URL links received
  • SMS alerts received before and after the debit
05

File an Official Cyber Crime Complaint

Visit cybercrime.gov.in and submit a formal complaint under "Report Financial Fraud". Upload all preserved screenshots, bank statements showing the deduction, and suspect details.

06

Secure Your Accounts and Change Passwords

From a clean, uncompromised device, change passwords for your primary email, net banking, and social accounts. Revoke third-party app authorizations and terminate active login sessions.

07

Watch Out for "Fund Recovery" Scammers

After suffering a loss, victims frequently turn to social media or search engines asking for help. Fraudsters monitor these inquiries and pose as "ethical hackers", "cyber advocates", or "recovery agents" claiming they can hack the fraudster and recover your money for an upfront fee.

Rule: Private third parties cannot freeze bank accounts or seize funds. Only official law enforcement agencies and banking compliance teams operating through verified legal channels have this authority. Never pay someone to recover stolen funds.
