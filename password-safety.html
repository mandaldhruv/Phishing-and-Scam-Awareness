Best Practices

The Three Pillars of Modern Password Security

1. Length Over Complexity

A 16-character passphrase composed of four unrelated memorable words (e.g., mango-winter-guitar-breeze) is vastly harder for supercomputers to crack than a complex but short 8-character string like Tr@7b!.

2. Never Reuse Passwords

When a secondary shopping site suffers a data leak, attackers test those leaked email-password pairs on thousands of banking, email, and social portals (known as Credential Stuffing). Always use unique passwords.

3. Use Password Managers

Humans cannot safely memorize dozens of 16-character distinct passwords. Modern open-source or reputable password managers (like Bitwarden, KeePassXC, or built-in OS keychains) encrypt your secrets locally.

Everyday Privacy

Digital Hygiene on Public Networks

When connecting to coffee shop, railway station, or airport Wi-Fi:

  • Avoid accessing net banking or financial portals: Open Wi-Fi routers can be monitored by malicious actors sitting nearby on the same subnet using packet analyzers.
  • Watch for "Evil Twin" networks: Attackers often configure Wi-Fi hotspots with names like "Free_Airport_WiFi" to trick passengers into routing all device traffic through their machine.
  • Keep mobile hotspot tethering disabled when not in use: Prevent unauthorized proximity pairing attempts.
  • Review app permissions periodically: Restrict location, microphone, and contacts access on apps that do not legitimately need them.
