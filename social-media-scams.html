Social Media and Messaging Traps

Social media platforms rely on social connections and familiarity, making them fertile ground for cloned profiles, urgent loan requests, and bogus job offers.

Threat Patterns

Prevalent Social Media Deceptions

From Instagram to Telegram, explore the mechanics of high-frequency social engineering scams.

Pattern 01

Account Cloning and Friend Impersonation

Scammers download photos and bio information from a public profile, create a near-identical username (such as adding an extra underscore), and friend all mutual contacts. They send messages claiming they are in a hospital emergency and need Rs. 5,000 sent via UPI immediately.

Pattern 02

Influencer Giveaways and Contests

Fake profiles impersonating popular creators or brands comment on posts: "You won a brand new iPhone 16! Direct message us to claim". When victims message them, the scammer asks for Rs. 850 "shipping insurance" or sends a malicious link to enter credit card details.

Pattern 03

Account Takeover and Link Relaying

A friend's compromised account messages you: "Hey, I am locked out of my account. Instagram is sending a link to your phone to help me get back in. Please send me the link you receive". In reality, the link is a password reset link for YOUR OWN account.

Pattern 04

The "Like and Subscribe" Telegram Trap

Unsolicited WhatsApp invites promise flexible income: "Like 3 YouTube videos and receive Rs. 150". You complete the trial and actually receive Rs. 150. You are then moved to a VIP Telegram group where you are persuaded to deposit Rs. 10,000 to unlock higher-tier tasks, after which withdrawals are permanently blocked.

Pattern 05

Fake Copyright Violation DMs

A direct message from "@Support_Badge_Help" warns: "Your account violated intellectual property rights and will be deleted in 24 hours. Fill this appeal form to keep your badge". The link leads to a phishing replica of the Instagram login screen.

Pattern 06

Romance and Friendship Schemes

Over weeks or months, a stranger builds an affectionate relationship online. Eventually, they claim they sent expensive gift packages stuck at customs, or have an urgent medical crisis, and persuade the victim to transfer substantial funds.

Protective Protocol

Checklist Before Trusting a Social Media Message

1 Verify the Profile Independently

Look closely at the handle. Does it contain duplicate letters, odd numbers, or recent creation dates? If someone claims to be a friend, check whether you already follow another account with the same name.

2 Confirm Emergency Money Requests via Direct Voice Call

Never send money based on a text or DM alone. Pick up your phone and call the friend or family member directly using their regular phone number stored in your contact book.

3 Never Forward Verification Links or Security Codes

If someone asks you to copy and paste a link or OTP sent to your SMS, they are attempting to hijack your account or register your phone number on their device.

4 Remember: Official Platforms Do Not Send Support Warnings in DMs

Instagram, Facebook, X, and LinkedIn deliver policy notices through official system notifications and registered account emails, never through direct chat messages.

5 Turn on Two-Factor Authentication via Authenticator Apps

Use authenticator apps (such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, or Aegis) rather than SMS where possible, making account takeovers significantly harder.
