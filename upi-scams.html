UPI and Digital Payment Safety

Unified Payments Interface (UPI) has made payments instant and accessible, but fraudsters exploit common misconceptions about how money enters and leaves your bank account.

Fundamental UPI Rule

Receiving money NEVER requires entering your UPI PIN.

If anyone asks you to open your payment app (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM), scan a QR code, or type your 4-digit or 6-digit UPI PIN to "accept", "receive", or "verify" an incoming refund or payment, it is an active scam.

Sending Money (PIN Required)

When You Send Money

  • You initiate or approve the transaction.
  • Your bank account balance will decrease.
  • You MUST enter your UPI PIN to authorize the debit.
  • The PIN serves as your digital signature to release funds.
Receiving Money (No PIN Required)

When You Receive Money

  • The sender sends money directly to your UPI ID or mobile number.
  • Your bank account balance increases automatically.
  • No action or PIN is required on your app.
  • You receive an SMS notification from your bank verifying the credit.
Deception Methods

Common Payment-Related Scam Scenarios

Understanding the specific tricks used by payment fraudsters helps you avoid accidental authorization.

Technique 01

The QR Code Receiving Trap

A scammer on an online classifieds site offers to buy your old furniture or phone. They claim their company account only supports payments via QR code. They send you an image of a QR code and say: "Scan this and enter your PIN to claim the payment."

Reality: Scanning and typing your PIN debits that exact amount from your account.

Technique 02

The Fake "Collect Request"

The attacker sends a UPI "Collect Request" for Rs. 5,000 to your UPI ID, with a remark saying: "Payment Received from Buyer - Tap to Accept". If you tap "Pay" or enter your PIN, the money is transferred to the attacker immediately.

Reality: Collect requests are payment demands, not incoming credits.

Technique 03

Spoofed Payment Screenshots

In retail stores or peer transactions, the fraudster shows a fake confirmation screen generated by a spoof app (such as fake Paytm or PhonePe screen generators) showing "Transaction Successful". They insist the SMS might be delayed and leave.

Reality: Never rely on the buyer's screen; check your own bank SMS or soundbox.

Technique 04

The "Accidental Money Sent" Scam

You receive an SMS spoofing your bank stating that Rs. 15,000 has been credited. Immediately, an agitated caller calls you claiming they sent the money to your number by mistake and begs you to return it via UPI immediately.

Reality: No real money was deposited; the initial SMS was fake or sent from an unauthorized number.

Technique 05

Customer Support Screen Sharing

When a transaction gets stuck as "Pending", victims search online for payment app customer support. The fake agent says: "To release the stuck amount, install AnyDesk from Play Store and enter Rs. 1 for verification."

Reality: The app allows the attacker to see your screen and read your incoming OTPs.

Technique 06

Fake Cashback Scratch Cards

Popups or WhatsApp messages display an animated scratch card claiming you won Rs. 1,999 cashback from Google Pay. Tapping the link opens a counterfeit payment page with a "Send to Bank" button that triggers a debit request.

Reality: Genuine cashbacks in payment apps are credited directly to your bank automatically.

Best Practices

Five Core Rules for UPI Protection

  • Never scan a QR code sent over chat: QR codes are designed for paying merchants, not for receiving funds.
  • Never share your UPI PIN: Your PIN must only ever be typed on the secure system keypad inside your official UPI application.
  • Always check the recipient name: Before confirming any payment, review the verified merchant or individual name displayed by the NPCI system.
  • Set reasonable daily transaction limits: Most banking apps allow you to set lower daily limits on UPI transfers to minimize potential exposure.
  • Never install remote desktop tools for payment help: Genuine payment apps have built-in in-app ticket support and will never ask you to install third-party screen apps.
